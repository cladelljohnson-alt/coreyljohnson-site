Black Male Authors to Read in 2026

By Corey L. Johnson  ·  September 23, 2026  ·  7 min read

Back to Blog

If you’re looking for Black male authors to read in 2026, the first question is what kind of story you want to spend time with. Do you want a love story? A mystery? Somebody making one bad decision and then spending the rest of the book trying to survive it?

Those are different reading moods. A list of names by itself doesn’t tell you much about which book belongs on your nightstand.

I write romance, psychological suspense, and Christian fiction. Black women are at the center of my romance because I want them to see themselves loved, pursued, and chosen. But this list reaches beyond romance. There are Black men writing about family, crime, faith, desire, and the things people do when the life they’ve built starts coming apart.

I’ve put together six Black male authors across literary fiction, crime, suspense, relationship fiction, and Christian fiction. Some have documented national bestsellers. Others may give you a name you haven’t come across yet. This is a reading guide, not a sales ranking.

The books include releases from 2024 through 2026, along with selections from my own catalog. I’ve labeled the dates because a book to read in 2026 doesn’t have to be a book first published in 2026.

And yes, I’m on the list. You’re on my website. You knew I was going to tell you about my books.

Percival Everett — for a familiar story told by someone else

Book to look up: James (2024; paperback edition released in 2026)
Reading mood: Literary and historical fiction

James is a No. 1 New York Times bestseller and a Pulitzer Prize winner. Those are good reasons to recognize the title. The premise helps you decide whether it’s your kind of book.

Everett retells Adventures of Huckleberry Finn from Jim’s point of view. When Jim learns that he may be sold and separated from his wife and daughter, he runs. The journey puts his own thinking, decisions, and fight for freedom at the center.

For readers interested in whose version of a story gets told, this is a place to start. Expect the brutality of slavery alongside dark humor. It’s a different reading experience from the romance and suspense picks below, and that range is part of why I wanted a broader list.

Read the publisher’s description and sample of James.

S. A. Cosby — when family trouble becomes everybody’s trouble

Book to look up: King of Ashes (2025)
Reading mood: Southern crime fiction and family drama

S. A. Cosby is a New York Times bestselling author. In King of Ashes, Roman Carruthers comes home after his father is seriously injured. His brother owes dangerous people money, and his sister has been carrying the weight of their family and its crematorium business. Roman knows finance. He thinks he can work out a solution.

The problem is that some people aren’t interested in a reasonable payment plan.

King of Ashes puts family loyalty inside a violent crime story. There’s also the question of what happened to the siblings’ mother, whose disappearance still hangs over the family. If you want a story about the cost of trying to rescue people you love, this is one to investigate. Expect darkness and violence alongside the family conflict.

Read the publisher’s description of King of Ashes.

Walter Mosley — for readers who want a detective with history

Recent book to look up: Gray Dawn (2025)
Reading mood: Detective fiction

Gray Dawn was an instant USA Today bestseller. It’s also the seventeenth novel in Walter Mosley’s Easy Rawlins series. That’s useful to know before you pick it up expecting a detective’s first case.

In 1970s Los Angeles, Easy has a thriving agency. Then Lutisha, a dangerous woman from his past, returns. The trouble reaches into both the criminal world and his personal life.

This is a recent release from an established author, with an established character whose past still has something to say about his present. If you enjoy following a detective across several books, Mosley gives you a series to explore. If you prefer to meet a character at the beginning, check the series order before starting here.

Read the publisher’s description of Gray Dawn.

Brandon Massey — when helping somebody opens the wrong door

Book to look up: Good Intentions (2025)
Reading mood: Psychological suspense

Nate Noble has a successful business and a fiancée. When his childhood friend Marvin Waters turns up after thirty years, Nate offers him a job. Helping an old friend sounds like a decent thing to do.

Marvin has a different understanding of their history.

The premise turns on an old grudge and the access that comes with being welcomed into someone’s life. That makes this a useful pick for readers who want the threat close to home: someone who knows you, remembers you, and has been carrying something you may not even realize needs explaining.

There’s a relationship in Nate’s life, but this belongs in the suspense part of your reading list. The central question is what his friend intends to do with that second chance.

Read Brandon Massey’s description of Good Intentions.

Lamartz Brown — when familiarity starts asking for commitment

Book to look up: Damaged By Necessity: A Stone Cold Love Story (2026)
Reading mood: Love and relationship fiction

Gina Thompson and A’Shai Blaze already know each other. They have a connection that has allowed comfort without commitment. Then that arrangement starts asking more of them.

“We’re fine the way we are” can only do so much work once feelings change.

Brown’s book follows that shift toward something deeper, with emotional wounds and the risk of honesty in the way. It’s a spin-off of Blank Canvas of You, so readers who like connected stories have another title to explore as well.

This is the relationship-focused stop on the list. If that’s the reading mood you came for, I’ve also put together a separate guide to Black male romance authors, with a book to look up for each writer.

Explore Lamartz Brown’s books and official purchase links.

Corey L. Johnson — faith, second chances, and unfinished business

From my catalog: The Grass Is Always Greener
Reading mood: Christian fiction and family drama

For my own place on this list, I want to start with The Grass Is Always Greener, the first book in my Rooted in Grace series. It’s a story of family secrets, grief, and second chances in the American South, with faith and resilience running through it.

The second book, The Seeds We Sow, follows Julie five years after she walked out of her own wedding. She has built a guarded new life in Memphis. Then Mark Baxter, the man she left behind, comes back into it. Faith, betrayal, forgiveness, and the cost of silence all have a place in that story.

If you’d rather start with romance, there’s Into You, about a wedding planner and a divorce attorney. Kiana Mercer and Darius have different relationships with the idea of happily ever after. That gives them plenty to work through.

And if you want suspense, you can look at They Never Came Home or The People-Pleaser in my book catalog.

You can also read more about why I write romance centered on Black women. That part of my work is personal, and I don’t mind saying so.

Pick the story you want to spend time with

You don’t have to want every kind of book on this list. Sometimes you want a detective. Sometimes you want a love story. Sometimes you want to see whether somebody can find their way back after making a mess of things.

Start with the description that makes you curious. Read a sample. See whether the voice works for you. A reading list should help you find your next book, and these names give you several places to begin.
